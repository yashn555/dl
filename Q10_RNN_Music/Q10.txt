----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PROBLEM STATEMENT (PS)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Build a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) to classify audio tracks into
10 music genres using the GTZAN dataset. The model processes audio
features (MFCCs) over time to capture the temporal dynamics and
rhythmic patterns characteristic of different genres.

Task type : 10-class audio classification
Input     : 3-second audio segment -> 130 frames of 13 MFCCs
Output    : probability distribution over 10 genres
Dataset   : GTZAN (10 genres, 100 tracks of 30s each = 1000 tracks)

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATASET FACTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Total: 1000 audio tracks (.wav), 30 seconds each, 22050 Hz sample rate.
Genres (10): blues, classical, country, disco, hiphop, jazz, metal, pop,
             reggae, rock. (100 tracks per Genre).
Known issue: `jazz.00054.wav` is corrupt in the original dataset and
             will be skipped gracefully by the extraction function.

Split strategy:
  The dataset is split 80/20 BY SONG (stratified).
  800 songs -> 8000 segments for training (with 15% val split).
  200 songs -> 2000 segments for testing.
  Splitting by song is CRITICAL: if we split by segment, consecutive
  3-second chunks of the same song would end up in both train and test,
  causing massive data leakage and artificially inflated accuracy.

--- Cell 4: MFCC extraction function and file inventory ---------------
extract_mfcc(path):
  - Loads audio with librosa (duration=30s).
  - Catches exceptions (e.g., the corrupt jazz.00054.wav) and returns None.
  - Slices the signal into 10 segments of 3 seconds.
  - Computes 13 MFCCs for each segment -> shape (13, 130).
  - Transposes to (130, 13) because RNNs expect (time_steps, features).
  - Returns a list of 10 matrices (one per segment).
Builds the master list of file paths and integer labels.

--- Cell 5: Train/Test split and feature extraction -------------------
train_test_split(..., stratify=labels) splits the FILE PATHS, not the
segments. This guarantees no song is in both train and test.
Iterates over train files, extracts MFCCs, and flattens the list of
segments into X_train (shape: N_segments, 130, 13).
Repeats for test files -> X_test.
Expected shapes: X_train ~ (8000, 130, 13), X_test ~ (2000, 130, 13).

--- Cell 6: Feature normalization -------------------------------------
StandardScaler is fitted on the TRAINING segments only.
The 3D tensor is temporarily flattened to (N, 130*13) for the scaler,
then reshaped back to (N, 130, 13).
Normalization is crucial for RNNs to prevent vanishing/exploding
gradients and to ensure all MFCC coefficients contribute equally.
Plots a raw MFCC spectrogram vs a normalized one for visual verification.

--- Cell 8: Model definition ------------------------------------------
Input((130, 13))          : 130 time steps, 13 features per step.
SimpleRNN(128, return_sequences=True) : processes the sequence, outputs 
                                        128 features at every time step.
SimpleRNN(64)             : processes the 128-dim sequence, outputs a
                            single 64-dim vector summarizing the 3s clip.
Dense(64, relu) + Dropout(0.3) : non-linear mixing and regularization.
Dense(10, softmax)        : 10-way classification.
Compiled with Adam and sparse_categorical_crossentropy.

--- Cell 10: Training -------------------------------------------------
validation_split=0.15 holds out 15% of the TRAINING segments for

EarlyStopping (patience=5) and ReduceLROnPlateau (patience=3) prevent
overfitting and refine convergence.

--- Cell 12: Segment-level evaluation ---------------------------------

--- Cell 13: Confusion matrix and classification report ---------------
Generates predictions for all test segments.
Prints precision, recall, F1 per genre.
Plots the confusion matrix. Typical confusions:
  - rock vs metal (similar instrumentation/distortion)
  - country vs blues (similar chord progressions/tempo)
  - classical vs jazz (complex harmonics, though classical is usually

--- Cell 15: Song-level prediction ------------------------------------
predict_song(path):
  - Extracts all 10 segments from the 30s track.
  - Normalizes them using the saved scaler.
  - Predicts probabilities for all 10 segments.
  - Averages the probabilities across the 10 segments (mean pooling).
  - Returns the genre with the highest average probability.
This is much more robust than predicting a single 3s clip, as it
integrates evidence from the entire track. Prints 5 test songs as a demo.

--- Cell 17: Save artifacts -------------------------------------------
Saves the Keras model and the scikit-learn scaler (as a pickle file)
to the `outputs/` directory. Both are required for inference on new
audio files later.

5. MODEL THEORY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Why SimpleRNN for audio?
    Audio is inherently sequential; the rhythm, tempo, and evolution of
    timbre over time define the genre. A standard Dense network treats
    each frame independently and loses temporal order. CNNs capture
    local spectrogram patterns but have limited receptive fields.
    RNNs maintain a hidden state that updates at every time step,
    effectively "remembering" the rhythmic context of the 3-second clip.
    SimpleRNN uses the equation: h_t = tanh(W_xh * x_t + W_hh * h_{t-1} + b).

5.2 MFCC (Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients)
    The raw audio waveform is too high-dimensional and noisy. MFCCs
    compress the audio into a compact representation that mimics human
    hearing:
    1. Frame the signal (short-time Fourier transform).
    2. Map frequencies to the Mel scale (logarithmic, perceptual).
    3. Take the log of the energies.
    4. Apply Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to decorrelate the bins.
    The result is 13 coefficients per frame representing the spectral
    envelope (timbre/instrumentation) of that 23ms window.

5.3 Input Shape and Many-to-One Architecture
    Input: (batch_size, 130 time_steps, 13 features).
    The first SimpleRNN returns a sequence of 130 hidden states.
    The second SimpleRNN returns only the FINAL hidden state (many-to-one),
    which acts as a fixed-size summary vector of the entire 3-second
    temporal evolution. This vector is passed to the Dense classifier.

5.4 Vanishing Gradient in SimpleRNN
    Because SimpleRNN multiplies the hidden state by the same weight
    matrix W_hh at every time step, gradients during Backpropagation
    Through Time (BPTT) can shrink exponentially (vanish) or explode.
    For 130 time steps, this makes learning long-term dependencies
    difficult. However, for 3-second audio clips capturing local rhythm
    and timbre transitions, SimpleRNN is often sufficient and trains
    much faster than gated variants like LSTM.

5.5 Song-level vs Segment-level Evaluation
    The model is trained and evaluated on 3-second segments. However,
    a 30-second song contains 10 segments. Predicting the genre of the
    whole song requires aggregating segment predictions. Averaging the
    softmax probabilities (mean pooling) is a simple and effective
    ensemble method that smooths out anomalies in individual segments
    (e.g., a quiet intro or a spoken-word break).

5.6 Data Leakage Prevention
    If we extracted all 10,000 segments first and then did a random
    80/20 split, segments from the SAME song would appear in both train
    and test sets. The model would memorize the specific song rather
    than learning general genre features, leading to ~95%+ test accuracy
    that fails completely on new music. Splitting the file paths BEFORE
    extraction guarantees strict song-level separation.

7. VIVA QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1. What is the GTZAN dataset?
    A standard benchmark for music genre classification containing 1000
    audio tracks of 30 seconds each, evenly divided into 10 genres.

Q2. Why did you split the data by song instead of by segment?
    To prevent data leakage. Consecutive 3-second segments of the same
    song are highly correlated. If they appear in both train and test
    sets, the model memorizes the song rather than learning genre
    features, resulting in artificially high test accuracy.

Q3. What are MFCCs and why use them?
    Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients represent the short-term power
    spectrum of audio on a perceptual Mel scale. They capture the
    timbre and instrumentation (the "texture" of the sound) while
    ignoring pitch, making them ideal for genre classification.

Q4. Why use a SimpleRNN instead of a Dense network?
    Dense networks ignore the temporal order of audio frames. SimpleRNNs
    maintain a hidden state that updates sequentially, allowing the
    network to capture rhythmic patterns and temporal evolution over
    the 3-second clip.

Q5. What is the vanishing gradient problem in SimpleRNN?
    During Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT), the gradient is multiplied
    by the recurrent weight matrix W_hh at every time step. If the
    eigenvalues of W_hh are less than 1, the gradient decays exponentially,
    making it hard for the network to learn long-range dependencies.

Q6. What is the shape of the input tensor?
    (batch_size, 130, 13). 130 time steps (frames) per 3-second segment,
    with 13 MFCC features per frame.

Q7. Why is the first RNN layer set to return_sequences=True?
    It outputs the hidden state at every time step, creating a sequence
    of 130 vectors. This allows the second RNN layer to process the
    temporal evolution at a higher level of abstraction.

Q8. How does the model predict the genre of a full 30-second song?
    It extracts 10 segments from the song, predicts the class
    probabilities for each segment, and averages them. The genre with
    the highest average probability is chosen (mean pooling).

Q9. Why normalize the MFCCs with StandardScaler?
    Different MFCC coefficients have different scales (e.g., the 0th
    coefficient represents overall energy and is much larger).
    Normalization ensures all features contribute equally to the
    gradients and stabilizes RNN training.

Q10. Which genres does the model confuse most, and why?
    Rock and Metal (similar distorted guitars and drum patterns), and
    Country and Blues (similar acoustic instrumentation and chord
    progressions). Classical is rarely confused because it lacks
    percussion and electronic elements.

Q11. What happens if a track is shorter than 30 seconds?
    The extraction function pads or truncates to 30s via librosa. If it
    is too short to form a full 3-second segment, that segment is
    skipped. The corrupt `jazz.00054.wav` file is caught by the
    try/except block and ignored.

Q12. Why use 13 MFCCs and not more?
    The first 13 coefficients capture the overall spectral envelope
    (timbre). Higher coefficients represent fine spectral details and
    noise, which are less relevant for broad genre classification and
    can introduce overfitting.

Q13. What is the difference between segment-level and song-level accuracy?
    Segment-level measures how often the model correctly classifies a
    random 3-second clip. Song-level measures how often it correctly
    classifies the entire track by aggregating evidence. Song-level
    accuracy is always higher and more practically useful.

Q14. How would you improve this SimpleRNN model?
    Replace SimpleRNN with LSTM or GRU to mitigate vanishing gradients
    and capture longer-term rhythmic structures. Alternatively, use a
    CNN-LSTM hybrid (CRNN) to capture local spectrogram patterns with
    Conv1D/Conv2D before passing them to the RNN.

Q15. What do the saved artifacts (model and scaler) do?
    The Keras model contains the trained RNN weights. The pickle file
    contains the mean and variance from the training data. Both are
    required to preprocess and classify new, unseen audio files
    correctly in production.
